# Results: one-map nonlinear point robot pilot

**2026-09-26 interpretation update:** Numerical results below are retained. See the [reassessment](inspection_20260926/REASSESSMENT.md) and [trajectory examples](inspection_20260926/trajectory_examples.png) for corrections to the risk-budget interpretation, CVaR formulation, and fairness of the training/refinement comparisons.

## Protocol

The reference run is `outputs/final/results.json`, generated by `python -m risk_diffusion.run --epochs 80 --output risk_diffusion/outputs/final` in the `diffuser` Conda environment. It trains a 2,500-route nominal prior and a 1,600-route risk-conditioned prior. Evaluation uses 12 held-out start-goal pairs, three paired diffusion sampling seeds, three risk budgets, and 2,048 independent stochastic executions per plan. Thus each ablation/budget has 36 plans and 73,728 stochastic executions. All reported plan success fractions compare Monte Carlo point estimates with the requested bound; exact binomial intervals for each plan are in the JSON. The approximate 95% cluster-bootstrap interval for B2 mean risk at Δ=.10 is 0.018–0.038 across the 12 tasks. Two more training seeds were run at radius 0.43, and separate experiments changed the obstacle radius to 0.35 and 0.52. These are three closely related geometries, not distinct Maze2D layouts.

The nominal model reaches the goal with about 98% probability after a common nominal endpoint correction, but its obstacle collision risk is substantial. The model therefore supplies a plausible yet weak nominal prior. The common correction is present in all methods, including B0.

## Ablation at requested Δ = 0.10

| Method | Mean execution risk | Plans meeting Δ | Mean path length | Mean hard violation CVaR | Planning s / 12-plan batch |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| B0 nominal | 0.410 | 17/36 | 3.69 | 0.120 | 0.082 |
| B1 deterministic SDF guidance | 0.368 | 19/36 | 3.70 | 0.099 | 0.143 |
| B2 chance guidance | 0.026 | 35/36 | 3.88 | -0.026 | 0.331 |
| B3 sampled CVaR guidance | 0.175 | 28/36 | 3.80 | 0.020 | 0.148 |
| B4 deterministic projection | 0.015 | 36/36 | 4.00 | -0.038 | 0.164 |
| B5 chance guidance + projection | 0.015 | 36/36 | 4.22 | -0.039 | 0.407 |
| B6 risk-conditioned prior | 0.568 | 10/36 | 3.91 | 0.179 | 0.086 |
| B7 risk-conditioned prior + chance | 0.021 | 36/36 | 4.13 | -0.025 | 0.341 |

The CVaR metric is signed; a negative value means the average loss in the worst 10% of executions is still below the hard constraint boundary. Planning time includes a batch of 12 plans, so it is not a per-plan latency. B3 uses 25 × 32 = 800 differentiable stochastic rollout draws per candidate batch. B2 uses covariance propagation and no sampled guidance rollouts. The evaluator uses 2,048 separate draws per plan for every method.

At Δ=.05, B2's mean realized risk is 0.023 and 35/36 plans meet the bound; B4 is 0.015 and 36/36. At Δ=.20, B2 is 0.033 and 35/36, while B4 stays at 0.015 and 36/36. These are safe outcomes on this particular noise level, but they show poor use of the available risk budget.

## Q1. Differentiable safety energy

The chance energy directly penalizes a sum of local Gaussian event probabilities exceeding Δ. Its gradient pushes the nominal path outward where radial obstacle risk or terminal goal tails dominate. The sampled CVaR gradient instead reduces the upper-tail magnitude of a smooth trajectory violation. It does not optimize event probability directly. B2 offers a clearer link to Δ and improves risk more at the default settings. B3 can improve when its strength increases.

## Q2. Accuracy of local Gaussian chance risk

The separate 100-route calibration set spans lower and upper obstacle clearances and independent measured risk from 0.010 to 0.794. The chance sum has Pearson correlation 0.998 with measured risk, mean absolute error 0.081, and mean signed overprediction 0.080. At threshold 0.10 it has zero false-safe routes and three conservative false-reject routes. High-risk sums may exceed one; those are surrogate bounds, not probabilities. The mild nonlinear drift and additive Gaussian noise in this testbed do not establish accuracy for strongly nonlinear or multimodal execution. Calibration error is dominated here by Boole-sum conservatism, not observed false safety.

## Q3. Chance versus CVaR

At Δ=.10, default B2 realizes 0.026 risk and B3 0.175. B3 is faster in this implementation despite 800 rollout draws because the rollout batch is small and the chance method differentiates sequential covariance updates. The strength sweep shows CVaR at λ=.32 reaches 0.059 mean risk, versus 0.136 at λ=.16 on the sweep's paired samples. Its worst-tail objective can work, but the default sample count gives only about 1.6 upper-tail draws at Δ=.05. That makes the small-Δ gradient noisy. No claim that CVaR is inherently inferior follows from this pilot.

## Q4. Projection

B4 achieves 0.015 risk at Δ=.10 at about half B2's batch planning time, but its path is 0.12 longer than B2's. B5 gains no measured risk reduction over B4 while adding 0.22 path length and about 0.24 s per batch. The projection acts on deterministic obstacle and goal violations; its stochastic success here results from low process noise and the wide corridor. It is not a risk guarantee.

The explicit nominal feasibility check passed all 108 B4 plans and all 108 B5 plans across three budgets, so this run observed zero projection failures. At Δ=.10, it passed 21/36 B0 plans, 36/36 B2, and 31/36 B3. The continuous-time path between discrete checks is not verified. Cross-seed mean control-array L2 distance was 2.58 for B0, 3.15 for B2, 2.82 for B4, and 3.60 for B5; this measures sample spread within the single upper route homotopy, not distinct route diversity.

## Q5–Q6. Risk-aware training and data

The conditional set mixes 800 ordinary routes with 800 lower-clearance routes. Independent 256-draw labels span measured risk 0–0.723. The conditional model receives measured risk at training time and requested Δ as its target at sampling time, with 15% condition dropout. B6 underperforms B0 and B7 has no material gain over B2. This evidence does not justify a risk-aware data pipeline here. It also does not prove that a stronger conditional architecture or better targeted data cannot help. Ordinary routes alone initially yielded labels only 0–0.075, which would have been inadequate for conditioning across the tested budgets; lower-clearance examples were necessary to cover the range. A dedicated risk-aware planner was unnecessary for generating labels in this pilot.

## Q7. Guidance strength

At Δ=.10, the chance sweep's mean risk falls from 0.279 at λ=0, to 0.248 at .01, 0.193 at .03, 0.103 at .10, 0.018 at .30, and 0.017 at .90. Mean path length rises from 3.77 to 4.05. The CVaR sweep falls from 0.279 at λ=0, to 0.227 at .06, 0.136 at .16, and 0.059 at .32. At CVaR λ=.32, changing K from 16 to 32 to 128 yielded mean risks 0.060, 0.059, and 0.072 on the sweep tasks. At chance λ=.10, changing γ from 5 to 15 to 40 yielded 0.103 in each case. These are small one-seed sensitivity checks; neither establishes insensitivity in general. The CVaR smooth-max/hinge temperature β remains fixed at 18.

## Q8. Does Δ control realized risk?

Only weakly. B2 mean execution risk is 0.023, 0.026, and 0.033 as requested Δ changes from .05 to .10 to .20. B4 stays at 0.015 because it has no Δ input. The chance energy is capable of changing with Δ, but the fixed λ drives plans well inside every bound. This pilot does not establish a well-calibrated risk-bounded planner that uses its requested budget efficiently.
