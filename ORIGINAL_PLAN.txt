Goal: Design and experimentally resolve a risk-bounded diffusion planner for stochastic trajectory planning

Design, implement, and evaluate the smallest convincing version of the risk-bounded diffusion planning framework described in the supplied presentation.

The high-level problem is:

$$ x_{k+1}=f(x_k,u_k,w_k) $$

Given an initial-state distribution, goal region, safe set/obstacles, horizon, and allowable trajectory risk \(\Delta\), generate controls \(u_{0:N-1}\) that achieve low cost while satisfying approximately

$$ P(x_{0:N-1}\in X_{\rm safe},\;x_N\in X_{\rm goal})\ge 1-\Delta. $$

The intended approach is to use a diffusion model as a trajectory/control prior and impose stochastic safety through some combination of:

nominal diffusion training \(p(u)\);
risk-conditioned training \(p(u\mid c)\);
chance-constraint energy guidance during denoising;
sample-based CVaR guidance during denoising;
projection/inpainting toward feasible trajectories.

The purpose of this project is not to assume all of these are useful. Determine experimentally which pieces are actually necessary and how they should be formulated.

1. First audit what we already have

Before writing a new planner, inspect the existing repository and prior project work for reusable components.

In particular, look for existing:

Diffuser/D4RL Maze2D code;
trajectory diffusion models;
Maze2D datasets and evaluation;
collision/SDF constraint code;
stochastic dynamics rollouts;
risk or Monte Carlo evaluators;
covariance propagation or chance-constraint code;
CVaR implementations;
gradient-guided diffusion;
projection/inpainting;
DAG-STL Maze2D components if useful;
previous execution-risk or DPCC experiments.

Reuse code when it cleanly matches this project, but do not contort an old project into this formulation merely because the code exists.

In particular, the prior DAG-STL Maze2D work may provide useful diffusion/data/environment infrastructure, but its state-space representation and temporal-logic machinery are not requirements here. This project does not need state plans, dwell constraints, temporal allocation, or execution-prefix reasoning.

Likewise, do not revive the previous FULL-vs-PREFIX DPCC hypothesis. That experiment addressed a different question and produced little horizon-matched safety difference.

At the end of this audit, explicitly state:

reuse / adapt / replace for each major subsystem and why.

2. Choose the smallest scientifically appropriate testbed

Prefer beginning with a controlled 2D Maze2D-style navigation environment rather than manipulator arms.

Maze2D is appropriate for rapidly answering whether the proposed guidance mechanisms work at all. However, the presentation specifically concerns stochastic nonlinear systems. Therefore:

use Maze2D or a similar 2D environment as the main development and ablation environment;
introduce stochastic execution dynamics rather than evaluating only deterministic trajectories;
if standard Maze2D dynamics are too simple to meaningfully test the local-linearization/chance-constraint method, add one minimal nonlinear 2D dynamical system rather than escalating immediately to a high-DOF manipulator.

Make this decision based on what each experiment needs to establish.

We need a clean scientific testbed, not impressive visuals.

3. Keep the diffusion representation compatible with risk reasoning

Decide explicitly what the diffusion model generates.

The slides are written in terms of control sequences \(u_{0:N-1}\), with stochastic dynamics producing states. That makes model-based stochastic rollout and differentiation conceptually clean.

Existing Diffuser-style implementations may instead generate joint state-action trajectories or state trajectories.

Determine which representation gives us the cleanest experiment:

$$ u_{0:N-1} $$

versus

$$ (x_0,u_0,x_1,u_1,\ldots,x_N) $$

versus an existing Maze2D trajectory representation.

Do not silently change the formulation. Document the choice and its consequences for dynamics consistency and risk gradients.

4. Establish a nominal diffusion baseline first

Build or reuse a standard trajectory diffusion model trained on ordinary feasible/collision-free planning data.

It should know nothing about stochastic risk beyond whatever nominal feasibility is implicit in its training data.

This is our baseline:

$$ p_\theta(u\mid x_0,x_{\rm goal},\text{environment}) $$

or the corresponding state-action formulation.

Verify that it can generate reasonable nominal plans before introducing risk.

Do not begin by building the full risk-conditioned training pipeline.

5. Build an independent stochastic execution evaluator

This component is critical.

Given a candidate trajectory/control sequence, run many stochastic executions under

$$ x_{k+1}=f(x_k,u_k,w_k) $$

and estimate:

whole-trajectory collision probability;
goal-reaching probability;
joint safe-and-goal probability;
trajectory cost;
violation magnitudes;
worst-tail losses/CVaR.

This high-sample evaluator is the approximate ground truth for experiments.

Keep it logically separate from whatever approximation guides diffusion. Otherwise we cannot tell whether a surrogate is actually accurate.

Use enough evaluation samples to provide uncertainty/confidence intervals for reported risk estimates.

6. Implement chance-constraint diffusion guidance

Implement the formulation proposed in the presentation.

At a denoising step, for the current candidate controls:

roll out the mean trajectory;
linearize the stochastic dynamics around the current trajectory;
propagate state covariance;
linearize obstacle/goal constraints;
approximate each violation probability with the Gaussian CDF;
aggregate risk using the proposed union-bound approximation;
compare estimated risk against \(\Delta\);
construct a smooth energy such as
$$ E_{\rm risk}(u) =\operatorname{softplus} \left( \gamma(\hat\Delta_{\rm UB}(u)-\Delta) \right); $$
guide denoising with
$$ s_{\rm guided} = s_\theta-\lambda\nabla_u E_{\rm risk}. $$

Use autodiff where possible instead of manually deriving fragile gradient expressions.

Instrument the method heavily. We need to know whether failure comes from covariance propagation, the Gaussian approximation, union-bound conservatism, poor gradients, or diffusion itself.

7. Implement sample-based CVaR guidance as a genuine alternative

For each candidate \(u\), execute \(K\) differentiable stochastic rollouts and define a trajectory-level loss such as

$$ l(x_{1:N}) = \max\{\text{obstacle violations},\text{goal violation}\}. $$

Safe execution should correspond to \(l\le0\).

Estimate

$$ CVaR_\Delta(l(u)) $$

using the standard sampled formulation and smooth the hinge/max where necessary.

Guide diffusion through

$$ -\lambda\nabla_u CVaR_\Delta(l(u)). $$

Keep the distinction clear:

chance guidance uses a local analytical approximation;

CVaR guidance uses stochastic execution samples and focuses on the bad tail.

Measure computational cost as well as safety.

8. Investigate projection/inpainting

Implement projection only after the two guidance approaches are working.

Define precisely what projection means in this environment. It should not be described vaguely as "make the trajectory safe."

Candidate formulations include projecting a denoised trajectory/control sequence toward a nearby dynamically feasible solution satisfying deterministic or risk-surrogate constraints, and then feeding that corrected sample back into subsequent denoising steps.

Test at least:

$$ \text{diffusion only} $$ $$ \text{diffusion + guidance} $$ $$ \text{diffusion + projection} $$ $$ \text{diffusion + guidance + projection}. $$

Measure whether projection contributes something that guidance alone does not, rather than assuming the combination is superior.

9. Only then test risk-aware training

The deck proposes learning \(p(u\mid c)\), where \(c\) can contain risk information.

Do not pay the cost of building this until inference-time guidance has been characterized.

Investigate practical ways to generate risk-aware training data:

ordinary planner trajectories followed by stochastic rollout labeling;
trajectories perturbed around nominal plans to create a useful range of risk;
plans generated by an existing model-based risk-aware planner;
mixtures of safe, marginal, and unsafe examples if useful for conditioning.

Determine what the conditioning variable should actually be:

requested \(\Delta\);
measured trajectory risk;
both;
another representation justified by the experiment.

If classifier-free guidance is used, implement a proper conditional/unconditional training setup.

Compare risk-aware training against the much simpler nominal-training + inference-guidance solution.

10. Answer the presentation's unresolved questions experimentally

The final result must give evidence-based answers to these questions:

Q1. What should the differentiable safety energy \(g(u)\) be?
Compare the chance-constraint surrogate and sampled CVaR formulation. Explain what each gradient is actually optimizing.

Q2. Is local Gaussian chance-constraint guidance sufficiently accurate for nonlinear stochastic execution?
Compare its predicted risk with high-sample Monte Carlo truth across many trajectories and risk levels. Measure calibration, bias, false-safe cases, and conservatism.

Q3. Does CVaR guidance improve safety or planning quality relative to chance guidance?
Compare safety, cost, solve time, sample count, robustness, and optimization behavior.

Q4. Does projection-based sampling materially help?
Determine whether it increases actual stochastic safety, merely deterministic feasibility, or mostly adds computational expense.

Q5. Is risk-aware diffusion training actually necessary?
Compare:

$$ p(u)+\text{risk guidance} $$

against

$$ p(u\mid c)+\text{risk guidance}. $$

Determine whether risk-aware training improves feasibility, number of denoising corrections, diversity, planning speed, or final safety enough to justify the extra training-data pipeline.

Q6. How should risk-aware training data be generated?
Experiment with the simplest viable approaches and identify whether Monte Carlo labeling of ordinary trajectories is enough or whether a dedicated risk-aware planner is needed.

Q7. How should guidance strength be chosen?
Sweep \(\lambda\), and where relevant \(\gamma,\beta,K\), and determine the tradeoff between safety, trajectory quality, diversity, and diffusion instability. Do not report a single hand-tuned successful value.

Q8. Does the requested risk level \(\Delta\) actually control realized risk?
Test several values of \(\Delta\). A risk-bounded planner should show meaningful correspondence between requested and realized risk rather than simply becoming "more cautious."

11. Required ablation matrix

At minimum evaluate:

B0: nominal diffusion.

B1: nominal diffusion + deterministic obstacle/SDF guidance, if applicable.

B2: nominal diffusion + chance-constraint guidance.

B3: nominal diffusion + CVaR guidance.

B4: nominal diffusion + projection.

B5: nominal diffusion + best risk guidance + projection.

B6: risk-conditioned diffusion without inference risk guidance.

B7: risk-conditioned diffusion + best inference risk guidance.

Not every variant needs to survive to the largest experiment. Run cheap pilots first and kill clearly unhelpful variants.

12. Evaluation

Report at least:

Monte-Carlo-estimated joint safety probability;
fraction of plans satisfying the requested risk bound;
goal success;
expected trajectory cost/path length;
worst-tail violation/CVaR;
risk-estimator calibration;
planning wall-clock time;
number of stochastic rollouts required;
number of diffusion steps;
projection/refinement failures;
trajectory diversity where relevant.

Use multiple environments/start-goal conditions and multiple random seeds.

When comparing safety, distinguish:

surrogate predicted safety from independently measured stochastic execution safety.

13. Important methodological rule

Do not let the project turn into:

"We added another generic cost to diffusion and observed fewer collisions."

The interesting technical question is whether a risk quantity tied to stochastic execution and an explicit risk budget \(\Delta\) can be represented accurately enough to guide a generative planner, and which combination of model-based approximation, sample-based tail risk, projection, and training conditioning is actually justified.

14. Literature and prior-work audit

As the method becomes concrete, continuously check the closest literature.

At minimum inspect the papers referenced in the supplied presentation:

Blackmore, Ono & Williams, Chance-Constrained Optimal Path Planning With Obstacles;
nonlinear/non-Gaussian risk-bounded trajectory optimization;
Risk-Guided Diffusion;
diffusion-based trajectory planning.

Also compare against directly relevant diffusion safety/planning methods already present in our research notes, including SafeDiffuser, MPD/constrained diffusion methods, DPCC where technically relevant, and conventional stochastic planners.

The purpose is both to reuse established machinery and to avoid accidentally claiming a standard ingredient as the contribution.

Do not stop the implementation merely because individual ingredients have precedent. Determine whether the resulting algorithmic combination or empirical capability creates a meaningful robotics contribution, using the conference-level novelty standard established in our other research work.

16. Work autonomously and simplify aggressively

Do not ask me to choose between Maze2D, a custom point robot, an old repository, state-only versus state-action diffusion, etc. Inspect what exists and choose the smallest setup capable of answering the scientific question.

Favor staged experiments over a large implementation.

If Maze2D cleanly answers a question, use Maze2D.

If a claim specifically depends on nonlinear stochastic dynamics and Maze2D cannot test it, add the smallest additional nonlinear system needed.

Do not jump to a manipulator merely to make the experiment appear more sophisticated.

The final objective is not to implement every box in the presentation. It is to determine, with controlled experiments, what a defensible risk-bounded diffusion planner should actually consist of.
